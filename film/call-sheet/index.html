The annotated call sheet

Every section, what it's for, and the question it answers. Download it as a PDF.

  1. Production. The film, the day of the shoot (Day 2 of 18), and who's in charge. Save the production office number.
  2. Times. Crew call is when most people start. Shooting call is when the first shot should roll. Lunch is six hours after crew call. Sunrise and sunset limit every exterior.
  3. Weather. Dress for it, and look for a rain plan in the notes.
  4. Locations. Each set, basecamp and crew parking. Park where it says, not at set.
  5. Nearest hospital. Know it before you need it.
  6. Scenes. What's shooting, in order: scene number, set, D (day) or N (night), page count, and cast numbers. A company move means the whole unit relocates.
  7. Cast. Each actor's number, character, status and times. Find an actor's scenes by their number.
  8. Background. How many extras, their call, where they're held and what to wear.
  9. Notes. What each department needs today, the rain plan and the radio channels.
  10. Crew calls. Your department's call. It may be earlier than crew call.
  11. Advance schedule. Tomorrow's scenes, call and weather, so you can plan tonight.

Codes

Cast status, from the AD's Day Out of Days: SW start work (first day) · W work · WF work finish (last day) · SWF start, work and finish the same day · H hold (paid, not working today).

Pages in eighths: a script page is eight eighths. 1 3/8 is one page and three eighths (eleven eighths). No scene counts as less than 1/8.
