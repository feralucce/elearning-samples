# Storyboard: Reading a Call Sheet

**Type:** job aid and micro-course, built as a site page in the Savage Light Studios style (`film/assets/sls.css`)
**Length:** about 10 minutes for Part 1; Part 2 is optional, about 10 more
**Status:** built 2026-10-02 as `index.html` with `annotated-call-sheet.pdf` (printed from `index.html?print`); not yet published. All four questions answered.
**Setting:** the same production as "Your First Day as a PA": *Last Light*, a non-union indie feature in the US. This is Day 2.

---

## 1. Why this sample

Everyone on a set gets the call sheet, and most new people read only one line of it: their call time.
The rest answers the questions they'll otherwise radio someone about: where to park, when lunch is, whether
it'll rain, which actors work today, whether there's a company move, and where the nearest hospital is.

A call sheet is a performance-support tool already, so the sample teaches people to use it rather than replacing it.
It also has a second audience: the people who make it. For an AD or producer, a call sheet is the day's plan made
public, and the mistakes on it cost the whole crew. That half makes this a project management sample as well.

## 2. Learners

| | Part 1: Reading it | Part 2: Making it (optional) |
|---|---|---|
| **Who** | Any new crew member: PAs, department crew, background, new actors | New ADs, coordinators, producers on a first feature |
| **Needs** | Find their own information fast, on a phone, before dawn | Check a draft before it goes out, so the crew can trust it |
| **Context** | Opens the sheet the night before and again at call | Builds it from the schedule, the crew list and the cast list |

## 3. Objectives

Part 1. By the end, the learner can:
1. Find their own call time, and tell crew call from shooting call.
2. Find where to go: basecamp, parking, set, and the nearest hospital.
3. Read the day's scenes: scene numbers, day or night, page counts in eighths, and cast numbers.
4. Read the cast list codes: SW, W, WF, SWF and H.
5. Spot the day's special notes: a company move, picture cars, stunts, weather, sunset.
6. Use the advance schedule to plan for tomorrow.

Part 2. By the end, the learner can:
7. Check a draft call sheet against the schedule and catch the errors a crew would hit.

## 4. Design

- **One call sheet, used three ways.** A full realistic call sheet for Day 2 is the centre of the page. It is
  the practice field, the job aid and the answer key.
- **Find-it tasks, not quizzes.** Each task asks a question someone really asks on set ("You're a grip. When do you
  need to be here?") and the learner taps the part of the sheet that answers it. Wrong taps explain what that
  part is instead, so every miss teaches a section.
- **Roles change the answer.** Some tasks are asked as different crew members, because the same sheet gives a grip,
  an actor and background different times.
- **The annotated sheet is the job aid.** After the tasks, the sheet flips to an annotated version with every section
  labelled. That version is also the printable PDF.
- **Part 2 is spot-the-error.** The learner gets the AD's draft of Day 3 and finds four mistakes, each a real kind of
  call-sheet error. It's optional, so Part 1 stands alone for crew.
- **Phone first.** The sheet is real HTML, not an image, so it reflows, zooms and reads aloud. Sections collapse on
  a phone, the way people actually skim them.

## 5. The call sheet (Day 2 of 18)

Built as HTML with these sections, top to bottom, like a standard two-page sheet:

1. **Header:** production, company, day 2 of 18, date, director, producer, 1st AD, 2nd AD and the production office number.
2. **Times:** crew call 6:00 a.m., shooting call 7:30 a.m., lunch 12:00 p.m. (6 hours from crew call), sunrise 6:58 a.m., sunset 6:51 p.m.
3. **Weather:** 40% chance of rain after 3 p.m., 52–64 °F.
4. **Locations:** Set 1 Main Street at Third Avenue; Set 2 Rosie's Diner, 410 Elm Street (company move after lunch);
   basecamp lot on Elm Street at Main; crew parking on the west side of the lot.
5. **Nearest hospital:** St. Agnes Hospital, 1200 Elm Street, 2.1 mi.
6. **Scenes:** four scenes with set, D/N, pages in eighths and cast numbers, plus the day's total pages.
7. **Cast:** five actors with number, name, character, status code, pickup, hair and makeup, and on-set times.
8. **Background:** 12 extras, call 7:00 a.m., holding area, wardrobe note.
9. **Notes by department:** picture car for scene 27, the stunt coordinator on set, the rain plan, walkie channels.
10. **Crew calls:** department by department. Grip and electric 5:30, PAs 5:30, camera 6:00, everyone else 6:00.
11. **Advance schedule:** Day 3, night exteriors, crew call 4:00 p.m.

## 6. Screens

### Title
- Slate: `Day 2 · Call Sheet · Take 1`. **Reading a Call Sheet.**
- Lede: "Your call time is one line. The other forty answer the questions you were about to ask."
- Buttons: **Start** and **Skip to the annotated sheet** (for people who want only the job aid).

### Part 1: Find it (8 tasks)
Each task shows the question, the role it's asked as, and the call sheet. The learner taps a section or a line.

| # | Asked as | Question | Answer on the sheet | Objective |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A grip | "When do I need to be here?" | Crew calls: grip and electric 5:30 a.m. (not crew call) | 1 |
| 2 | Anyone | "When does the camera roll for the first time?" | Shooting call 7:30 a.m. | 1 |
| 3 | A new crew member | "Where do I park?" | Crew parking: west side of the lot | 2 |
| 4 | Anyone | "Someone's hurt. Where's the hospital?" | Nearest hospital | 2 |
| 5 | Jules (cast #1) | "Which scenes am I in today?" | Scenes with cast 1 | 3 |
| 6 | Anyone | "Whose first day is it?" | The actor marked SW | 4 |
| 7 | A PA | "Is anything moving today?" | Company move to Rosie's Diner after lunch | 5 |
| 8 | Anyone | "Do I need to bring rain gear tomorrow?" | Today's weather and the Day 3 advance schedule (night exteriors) | 5, 6 |

Feedback is one line: what the part they tapped is for, and where the answer is if they missed.

### The annotated sheet (job aid)
- The same sheet with numbered labels on every section and a short key: what it is, who reads it, and the
  question it answers.
- A **codes** box: SW (start work), W (work), WF (work finish), SWF (start, work and finish the same day),
  H (hold: paid, not working that day). The codes come from the AD's Day Out of Days. Pages in eighths: a page is
  eight eighths, 1 3/8 is eleven eighths, and no scene is counted as less than 1/8.
- Button: **Download the annotated call sheet (PDF)**.

### Part 2: Check the draft (optional)
- Lede: "The 2nd AD sends you the draft for Day 3 at 7 p.m. Find what's wrong before it goes to the crew."
- The Day 3 draft has four planted errors. The learner taps each one; a counter shows 0 / 4.
  1. **A cast member is in a scene but not on the cast list.** The scene lists cast 4; the cast list has no 4.
  2. **Exterior scenes are scheduled after sunset**, labelled D (day).
  3. **Crew call is under ten hours after yesterday's wrap.** Day 2 wrapped at 7:00 p.m.; Day 3's draft call is 4:00 a.m.
     (It should be 4:00 p.m., as the advance schedule said.)
  4. **The nearest hospital is missing** for a new location.
- After each find: why it matters to the crew, in one line.
- Closing note: "A call sheet is the day's plan made public. Check it against the schedule, the cast list and
  yesterday's wrap before it goes out."

### Close
- What you can do now (the objectives in plain words).
- Links: the PDF job aid, "Your First Day as a PA", and the samples index.
