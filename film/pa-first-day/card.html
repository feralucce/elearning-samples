The PA's First-Day Card

Everything from "Your First Day as a PA" on one page. Fold it into your back pocket.

Before call

  • Your call time is on your line of the call sheet. PAs are often earlier than general crew call.
  • Arrive 15 minutes early. Early is on time.
  • Find the nearest hospital on the sheet before you need it.
  • Sign in with the key PA and ask where they want you.

Channel 1 is production. Keep it to one sentence.

The lockup

When someone wants through while you're rolling:

"We're rolling right now. It'll be about a minute. Thanks so much for waiting."
  • A lockup is a request. Never touch anyone.
  • Echo "rolling" and "cut" on the radio.
  • Let them through on "cut", and thank them.

Who you work for

  • You work for the ADs. Take direction from the 2nd AD and the key PA.
  • Other departments' gear is theirs. Don't touch it unless they ask and show you how.
  • Messages for the director go through the ADs.
  • Made a mistake? Own it in one sentence and say what you'll do next time.

Safety: call it first

  • Anyone on set can call a hold for safety. Call it, then fix it.
  • On channel 1: "Hold the roll, [what and where]."
  • A lost minute is cheap. An injury stops the shoot.
  • After a near miss, tell the truth fast.

Before you leave

  • Walkie and earpiece back to the key PA
  • Cones and signs picked up from your post
  • Tomorrow's call sheet in hand
  • Signed out with the key PA

Where to stand

  • Out of frame: if you can see the lens, it may see you.
  • Out of the eyeline: never where the actor looks, and don't watch them during a take.
  • Eyes on the approach, so you see people before they reach the shot.
  • Not sure? Ask "Am I clear?"
