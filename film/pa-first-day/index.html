Your First Day as a PA

Call time is 6 a.m. Nobody's going to explain twice.

A branching scenario, about 15 minutes. A non-union indie feature in the US.

BriefingBefore call

How today works

What you'll practise: reading a call sheet, talking on the radio, holding a lockup, standing clear of the frame and the actors' eyelines, knowing who you work for, calling a safety hold, and wrapping out.

You'll make the calls a new production assistant makes on a real shooting day. Three meters track how it's going:

  • Crew trust: would they work with you again?
  • Schedule: did you help the day run on time?
  • Safety: did everyone go home in one piece? Low safety decides your ending on its own.

You can't get fired. Mistakes get corrected, like on a real set. There's a debrief at the end, and a card to keep.

Scene 2The Radio6:15 a.m.

The radio

Marco hands you a walkie and an earpiece. "Production's on channel 1. Don't talk on it unless you have to."

Dana: Marco, Dana.

Marco's guiding a truck back and doesn't answer. Dana is the 2nd AD, and your boss today. Ten seconds later:

Dana: Anyone near basecamp?

You're at basecamp. What do you say?

  1. "Go for [your name], I'm at basecamp."

    Dana: "Need the sides for Jules in hair and makeup." You: "Copy." You take the route on the basecamp map.

    (+10, +5, 0)

  2. "Hi Dana, this is the new PA, I'm at basecamp right now, what do you need me to do?"

    Dana: "…Need sides in makeup. Keep it short on 1."

    (0, −5, 0)

  3. Wait for Marco to answer.

    Dana, again: "Anyone?" Marco finally answers and gives you a look.

    (−5, −5, 0)

Scene 3The Lockup9:20 a.m.

The lockup: where do you stand?

Marco puts you on the corner of Main and Third. "Nobody walks into the shot while we're rolling. Find a spot where you can see anyone coming. I'll be back."

They're lighting a shot of Jules, the lead. Jules plays the scene to the other actor, who stands just beside the lens.

Where do you stand?

  1. At the corner, behind the camera's side, with a clear view up the avenue

    Marco, passing: "Good spot."

    (+5, +5, 0)

  2. Beside the camera, so you can hear the AD

    Jules stops mid-line. Dana: "Whoever's by camera, you're in Jules's eyeline. Clear it, please." The eyeline is lit on the map.

    (−10, −5, 0)

  3. Halfway down the block, closer to where people come from

    The camera operator: "There's someone in the back of frame." You're the someone. The frame is lit on the map.

    (−5, −10, 0)
On setAn actor's eyeline is where they look during the shot, usually at the other actor beside the lens. Never stand in it, and don't watch an actor during a take. If you can see the lens, it may see you. When in doubt, ask "Am I clear?"
Scene 3The Lockup9:30 a.m.

The lockup: the neighbour

You're at the corner, out of frame and out of the eyeline.

Dana: Lock it up… Rolling!

A neighbour with grocery bags comes down the avenue to your corner. "I live right there. I just need to get through."

What do you do?

  1. "We're rolling right now. It'll be about a minute. Thanks so much for waiting." Then echo "rolling" on the radio.

    You hear "cut", and you walk them through. They thank you.

    (+10, +5, 0)

  2. Let them through. It's their street.

    They walk into the back of the shot. Radio: "Cut! Who's got that corner?"

    (−15, −15, 0) · goes to the branch scene

  3. Put out an arm and block them.

    They push past you, angry. Radio: "Cut!" You've touched a member of the public.

    (−10, 0, −5) · goes to the branch scene
On setA lockup is a request, not an order. You can't legally stop the public, so be polite, give a time, and thank them. Never touch anyone. Echo "rolling" and "cut" so the next PA hears it too.

Branch scene: only if the neighbour walked into the shot

Walk and talk

Dana walks up. "What happened on your corner?"

What do you say?

  1. "That was my corner. My mistake. Next time I'll ask them to wait and tell them how long."

    Dana: "Good. It happens once."

    (+10, 0, 0)

  2. "They said they lived there. I didn't think I could stop them."

    Dana: "You can't stop them. You can ask nicely and tell them how long."

    (0, 0, 0)

  3. "Nobody told me what to do."

    Dana: "Marco told you. Next time, ask if you're not sure."

On setOwn the mistake in one sentence and say what you'll do next time. ADs remember the answer more than the mistake.
Scene 4Between Setups11:00 a.m.

Two requests at once

The crew is turning around for the reverse. Two requests reach you at the same moment.

Rae, a grip: "Hey, can you grab that C-stand and move it over by the truck?"

Jules: "Could you tell the director I want to try the line differently?"

What do you do?

  1. Tell Rae you'll check with Dana whether you're free to help. Tell Jules you'll pass it to the 2nd AD. Then radio Dana.

    Dana: "Copy, I'll tell the 1st. Rae can have you for five." Rae shows you how they want the stand carried.

    (+10, 0, +5)

  2. Move the C-stand yourself, then walk over to the director.

    Rae: "Not like that. Don't touch my gear without asking." The 1st AD stops you before you reach the director.

    (−10, −5, −5)

  3. Tell them both you're busy.

    Jules has to find someone else, and Rae carries it alone.

    (−5, 0, 0)
On setYou work for the AD department. Other departments' gear belongs to them: don't touch it unless they ask and show you how. Messages for the director go through the ADs.
Scene 5The Picture Car2:15 p.m.

The picture car

After lunch, the scene is a car pulling up fast to the kerb. The precision driver is rehearsing. You're holding the background extras on the sidewalk.

The car is backing up to its first mark. One extra steps off the kerb to take a selfie, right into its path. From camera, nobody can see them.

What do you do?

  1. On channel 1: "Hold the roll, person in the car's path!" Then move the extra back.

    Dana: "Holding! Thank you." The car stops. The stunt coordinator moves the extras' holding area back from the kerb.

    (+10, −5, +20)

  2. Walk over and quietly ask the extra to step back.

    The car stops a few feet from them. The driver is shaken, and the stunt coordinator wants to know why nobody called it.

    (0, 0, −10) · goes to the branch scene

  3. It's only a rehearsal and the driver can see. Say nothing.

    The driver can't see behind the car. Someone shouts. The car stops with inches to spare.

    (−10, 0, −25) · goes to the branch scene
On setAnyone on set can call a hold for safety, and nobody will be angry that you did. Call it first, then fix it. A lost minute is cheap; an injury shuts down the shoot.

Branch scene: only if the hold wasn't called

Safety meeting

Nobody was hurt. The 1st AD stops work and calls the crew together. "Who had eyes on the sidewalk?"

What do you do?

  1. "I did. I saw them step off, and I should have called a hold."

    1st AD: "Thank you. Next time, call it. Everyone hear that? Anyone can call it."

    (+10, 0, +10)

  2. Say nothing.

    The extra points at you.

    (−15, 0, −5)
On setAfter a near miss, tell the truth fast. The safety meeting exists so it doesn't happen twice.
