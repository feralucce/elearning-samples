# Storyboard: Set Safety Orientation, with an Evaluation Plan

**Type:** compliance-style orientation built as a site page in the Savage Light Studios style, plus a
separate evaluation plan page (and PDF) using the four Kirkpatrick levels
**Length:** about 15 minutes
**Status:** built 2026-10-02: `index.html` (from `index.src.html` + `diner-map.svg` via `build.py`), `evaluation-plan.html` and `evaluation-plan.pdf`; not yet published. All four questions answered.
**Setting:** *Last Light*, the same non-union indie feature in the US. Everyone takes it before their first day.

---

## 1. Why this sample

On a small shoot, safety training is usually the 1st AD talking for two minutes at call. It's the closest film
gets to corporate compliance training, and it's where training most obviously has to change behaviour, not
just knowledge: a crew that can recite the rules but still runs cable across a walkway has learned nothing.

That makes it the right place for the evaluation plan. The orientation is the course; the plan says how
we'd know it worked, at all four levels, using things a production can actually count.

## 2. Learners

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Everyone working on the show: crew of every department, cast, and background on their first day. |
| **Know already** | Varies widely. Grips and electricians know their gear; PAs, cast and background often know nothing. |
| **Need** | The handful of rules that prevent most injuries, the words the crew shouts, and permission to stop work. |
| **Context** | Taken on a phone before day 1, in one sitting. Reviewed again at the daily safety meeting. |

## 3. Objectives

By the end, the learner can:
1. Say who's responsible for safety (everyone) and who runs it (the 1st AD), and call a hold for safety.
2. Recognise the common hazards on a set: cable, stands, overhead work, ladders, electrical, hot lights, vehicles, weather and fatigue.
3. Respond to the calls the crew shouts ("Points", "Striking", "Watch your back") and use them.
4. Know where the first aid kit, the set medic and the nearest hospital are, and report every incident and near miss.
5. Stay clear of specialist work they're not part of: stunts, picture cars, special effects and prop weapons.

## 4. Design

- **Short lessons, one per hazard group,** each ending on one rule in a single line.
- **A hazard hunt.** A top-down map of the diner set (Set 2 from the call sheet) in the Backwater Static map style.
  The learner finds eight hazards hidden in a normal-looking set: tapping one reveals what's wrong and the fix.
  It's the practice, and it's also the Level 2 measure.
- **Three quick scenarios,** the PA scenario's format but one decision each: a stinger across the walkway, a
  wobbling stand, and the drive home after a 14-hour day.
- **Knowledge check:** 8 questions, 80% to pass, with feedback that teaches.
- **Acknowledgement:** a final screen where the learner confirms they've read the rules and know they can call a hold.
  (In this sample nothing is recorded.)
- **The evaluation plan is a separate page,** written for the producer and 1st AD, so a reviewer can read it
  without taking the course.

## 5. The course

### Title
- Slate: `Before Day 1 · Safety · Take 1`. **Set Safety Orientation.**
- Lede: "Anyone can call a hold for safety. Here's when, and the eight other things that keep a crew in one piece."

### Lesson 1: Everyone's job
- The 1st AD runs safety and holds a safety meeting at call every day, and before anything unusual (stunts,
  cars, effects, weapons, heights, water).
- Anyone can call **"Hold the roll for safety."** No one will be angry you called it.
- **Rule:** see it, call it, then fix it or get the person who can.

### Lesson 2: Walkways, cable and stands
- Cable runs: along walls, never across a walkway without a mat or a cable ramp, taped down.
- Stands: point the high leg under the load and saddle a sandbag over it. Any stand with an arm out gets a bag, more for wind or a heavy light. Don't move another department's stand.
- **Rule:** if you'd trip on it in the dark, so will someone else.

### Lesson 3: Overhead, ladders and lifting
- "Points!" (or "Hot points!") means someone is carrying gear with sharp or pointed ends through, such as stands, tripods or dolly track. Stop, look, and step aside.
- "Watch your back!" means someone is coming through behind you with something that could hit you.
- Ladders: one person on it, one footing it. Nobody walks under work overhead.
- Lift with your legs, ask for a second person, use the cart.
- **Rule:** look up when you walk onto a set.

### Lesson 4: Electrical and lights
- Only electricians touch the generator, the distribution and the connections. The generator has a keep-clear zone.
- "Striking!" means a light is about to be switched on or off. Look away from it.
- Lights stay hot long after they're switched off. Gloves, or leave it.
- Water and electricity: rain plan means cables off the ground and covered.
- **Rule:** if it's plugged in and it's not your department's, don't touch it.

### Lesson 5: Specialist work
- Stunts, picture cars, special effects and prop weapons happen only with their specialist present and a safety meeting first.
- Keep clear of the action area and the vehicle path; never stand behind a reversing car.
- Prop weapons: only the armourer hands them out, takes them back and checks them. Treat every one as real.
- **Rule:** if it's specialist work, you're either part of it or out of it.

### Lesson 6: People: fatigue, weather and the drive home
- Turnaround is at least 10 hours between wrap and the next call. Long days add up.
- Too tired to drive? Say so. Productions arrange a ride or a room; it's cheaper than a crash.
- Heat, cold and rain: water, layers, breaks. Lightning stops exterior work.
- **Rule:** the most dangerous part of a shoot day is often the drive home.

### Lesson 7: When something happens
- First aid kit: at the production table at basecamp and on the set cart. Set medic, if there is one, is on the call sheet.
- Nearest hospital: on every call sheet.
- Report every incident **and every near miss** to the 1st AD the same day. Near misses are how the next accident is prevented.
- **Rule:** nobody gets in trouble for reporting.

### Hazard hunt (Set 2: Rosie's Diner)
A top-down map of the diner with the camera, lights, a dolly track, the crew and the kitchen. Eight hazards to find:

| # | Hazard | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A cable crossing the doorway with no mat | Run it along the wall or cover it with a mat |
| 2 | A C-stand with an arm out and no sandbag | High leg under the load, sandbag on it |
| 3 | A ladder in use with no one footing it | A second person foots it |
| 4 | A stinger lying in water from the kitchen sink | Lift it off the floor and call electric |
| 5 | A hot light at head height in the walkway | Move it, or flag and mark it |
| 6 | The fire exit blocked by equipment cases | Keep exits clear |
| 7 | A crew member sitting on the dolly track | Off the track; it's not a bench |
| 8 | The first aid kit buried under a pile of jackets | Clear and visible, every set |

Counter: "Found 0 of 8". Each find shows the fix in one line. After 8, or on "Show me", all are marked.

### Three quick calls
1. **The stinger across the walkway.** You're carrying coffee and there's an untaped cable across the door. A: step over it and tell no one. B: **put the coffee down, tell electric on the radio**, and stand by it until it's covered. C: move it yourself.
2. **The wobbling stand.** A stand with a light on it rocks when the door opens. A: hold it and **call grip**. B: add your own sandbag on any leg. C: ignore it; it's grip's.
3. **The drive home.** Wrap is at 9 p.m. after 14 hours, and you're an hour from home. A: drive with the window down. B: **tell the 2nd AD you're too tired to drive.** C: nap in the car and then drive.

### Knowledge check (8 questions, 80% to pass)
One per lesson, plus one on the hazard hunt and one on the calls ("What does 'Striking!' mean?").

### Acknowledgement
"I've read the set safety rules, and I know I can call a hold for safety at any time." Button: **I understand**.
Then a closing screen with links to the evaluation plan, the PA scenario and the call sheet course.

## 6. The evaluation plan (separate page and PDF)

Written for the producer and 1st AD of *Last Light*. **Owner: the 1st AD, reporting to the producer.** One table per level, then a timeline.

| Level | Question | Measure | Source | When | Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 Reaction** | Did people find it useful and relevant? | 3-question survey: relevance, clarity, confidence to call a hold (1–5) | End of the course | Before day 1 | Average 4 or more on "confident to call a hold" |
| **2 Learning** | Do they know the rules? | Knowledge check score; hazard hunt finds without hints | The course | Before day 1 | 100% pass at 80%; average 6 of 8 hazards found first try |
| **3 Behaviour** | Do they do it on set? | Observation checklist by the 1st AD and key PA: cable runs covered, stands bagged, ladders footed, exits clear; holds called for safety | Set walk at call and after each company move | Days 1, 3 and 10 | 90% of checklist items met by day 3 |
| **4 Results** | Is the set safer? | Recordable injuries; near misses reported; time lost to safety incidents | Incident and near-miss log; production reports | Weekly, then at wrap | Zero lost-time injuries; near-miss reports **up** in week 1 (people reporting), then down |

Notes in the plan:
- **Why near misses should rise first.** A training that works makes people report more, not less, at first.
  A falling number in week 1 would mean people aren't reporting.
- **Isolating the training's effect.** On one show there's no control group. Compare against the production
  company's previous show if it kept a log, and look at whether the observed behaviours match the trained ones.
- **Cost.** Everything uses tools a production already has: the course, the call sheet, a printed checklist and
  the incident log. The 1st AD's set walk takes 10 minutes.
- **Acting on it.** If a checklist item keeps failing, the 1st AD covers it at the next safety meeting and the
  course lesson gets rewritten.
